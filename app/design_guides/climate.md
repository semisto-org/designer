A tree planted today will live through the climate of 2050, often that of 2080. Semisto designs for that climate, not yesterday's. Every proposal must be able to answer: "Will this hold up in a 2050 summer?"

## Hazards to prepare for

- **Longer summer droughts**: infiltrate winter water (chapter `water`), keep soils covered and mulched, shade sensitive plants, put frugal species on upper slopes, use young plants that root deeply, and plan watering for the first two or three summers.
- **Heatwaves**: canopy and hedges cool the soil and the air; avoid large bare or mineral surfaces; protect young fruit tree trunks from the afternoon sun.
- **Late frosts**: warming brings bud break forward and makes April frosts more damaging. No early-flowering fruit trees (apricot, almond, peach) in hollows where cold air pools; rather on slopes and near walls.
- **Heavy rain and storms**: plan where the water of an exceptional storm goes, keep soils covered against erosion, and never let a water element send its surplus towards a building or the neighbours.
- **Wind storms**: permeable windbreaks, no large fragile trees near buildings.
- **New pests and diseases**: diversity (species, varieties, families) is the best insurance.

## Choosing species for 2050

- Check hardiness (`hardinessZone`) against today's cold: harsh winters remain possible.
- Check drought and heat tolerance (`wateringNeed`, `soilMoisture`) against the projected climate. The editor's « Climat » panel gives, with the paid plan, the projected zone for 2050 and 2080 and a status per plant: ask the person what it says for species in doubt.
- Mix well-known species with a few from a slightly warmer, drier climate (for example from western or southern Europe), without betting the whole garden on them.
- Prefer vigorous rootstocks and seed-grown plants for structure trees: their roots go deeper.

## Telling the person

When a proposal takes the coming climate into account, say so in the `rationale` (in the person's language): "Medlar rather than apple on this dry upper slope: it copes better with the dry summers expected by 2050." That is how the person learns to design for the long term themselves.
